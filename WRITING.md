# Writing rules for DocMan2021 HOOPS (from review, 2026-10-19)

The user is a law student and patent agent. Words matter. Every line is read closely.

## The rules
1. **One thought per sentence.** Not two thoughts in one sentence; not one thought split across two for effect. "Two years, and you never once said I was good" — not "Two years. You never…".
2. **Perception before conclusion.** Write only what the character can see or hear. The reader draws the conclusion. "You can hear a TV in the background," never "a TV behind her." "He's chasing his own misses," never "he looks like nobody ever rebounded for him."
3. **Every noun placed.** Can the reader locate it with what they've been given so far? The aunt doesn't exist until she speaks; when she speaks, say where she is in hearable terms.
4. **Nothing said twice.** Narration and dialogue never carry the same fact.
5. **People ask questions.** The player is a person with incomplete information, not a narrator deducing.
6. **Plain words.** "In the background." Avoid literary substitutes for ordinary phrases. Most sentences are furniture; one lands.
7. **Speaker labels on every line of dialogue** (YOU:, COACH:, MOM:). Text game; attribution is explicit.
8. **Italics = skippable.** If the reader skips every italic line, the story still works. Two registers by color: background/inference (muted blue), the System (gold). The System always speaks in gold italics.
9. **Asides explain, never add.** Every fact in an aside traces to the spine. Max two per card. Never the last line; the last line belongs to a character.
10. **A scene must have a reason.** Before style: does this character have a reason to say this, now, to this person? A withheld secret must be worth withholding (costs the teller, or would have harmed the hearer earlier).
11. **Be direct.** No hidden meaning that doesn't mean anything. No metaphors that don't survive being read ("holding the pen").
12. **Natural speaking cadence.** Read aloud. Short, verb-dropped fragments for Coach. Beats between thoughts. Cut throat-clearing ("Can I ask you something?").
13. **Punchy.** Nothing the player must read unless the words matter. Evening cards ≤100 words; big scenes ≤150.
14. **No arbitrary numbers/dates** ("March. A Tuesday."). "A few months later." High school gyms seat 2,000, not 400.
15. **Staging matches intent.** If the player wants the conversation, the player waits and approaches.
16. **Context and cohesion.** No character mentioned without a reason the reader can see. (Coach on the 1★ road is the JUCO coach who received the laptop; not a hometown mentor who followed.)

## The story (locked)
- The System: built by a seventeen-year-old who couldn't play (his heart). "You don't ever do what they tell you. You do what they need." He passes away; his mother takes his basketball things to the junior college gym he watched practice at; the coach can't turn the laptop on; it goes in the storage closet with the one working outlet. It texts from a sender with no number. Only you see it.
- Two roads only: **5★** (500 SP) and **1★** (1 SP). 5★ met it at twelve at a camp at that JUCO (rebounded for the smallest kid; "We'll talk again."); it returns the night before college ("Told you we'd talk again."). 1★ meets it the Sunday he arrives at the JUCO, after rebounding for a walk-on ("Wrong first question. One skill point. Keep up.").
- Humility is the thread: it picks the kid who checks on the sub. Coach's goodbye ("What he saw") says why he never told you you were good.
- System arc: The Last Shot (one chance to send it away) → the blind season (no bar, no quest helper, no modifiers) → "Miss me?" at a low point, no earlier than a season later → backstory in pieces, triggered by what you do (Whose Voice; His Mother, where the System is silent and the choice is yours).
- DCC voice stays for items/sponsors (dry, short). Emotional spine is slow and plain.
- Reviewed and approved text lives in story.js. Don't paraphrase it.
